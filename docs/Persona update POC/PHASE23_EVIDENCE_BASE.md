# Phase 23 — Evidence Base (Phase 10A–22 Consolidation)

**Zero API calls made to produce this document.** This is a synthesis of already-published Phase 10A–22 documents and CSVs. No historical artifact, production file, mapping, resolver, renderer, persona data, or engine logic was modified. No new experiment was run.

Terminology preserved throughout: **Question A** = retrieval completeness (is the fact selectable/present in context); **Question B** = model behavior/use of retrieved information; **B** = existing structured rendering; **L** = full labeled-scalar diagnostic (all six top-level scalars labeled, Phase 16); **LA** = age-only labeled-scalar diagnostic (Phase 20); **FS/FR/SS/SR** = Full-Structured/Full-Raw/Selective-Structured/Selective-Raw (Phase 10B/10D matrix); **H1/H2/H3** = the age-specific / broader-labeling / generation-variance hypotheses from Phases 20–22.

## 1. Retrieval / Question-A evidence

The retrieval side of this program resolved cleanly and is now closed for the scenarios tested:

- **Phase 10A** established that the historical "A" condition never represented "full raw document" — it was an accidental null-provider fallback to a short, fact-free sketch. This invalidated the A-vs-D "structure vs. volume" conclusion from Phase 7/9 while leaving B/C/D's own real-provider results untouched.
- **The corrected 2×2 matrix** (FS=reused D, SS=B, SR=new "X", FR=new) let Phase 10D cleanly separate selection from format for the first time: a ~50-point factuality swing from selection (FS 96.7% / SS 46.7%) versus only a ~3-point swing from format (FS 96.7% vs FR 93.3%; SS 46.7% vs SR 43.3%).
- **Phase 10D's** 23/23 SS/SR failures all traced to three specific, nameable field-mapping gaps (family, friends, location) — not to a diffuse weakness of selective retrieval as an architecture.
- **Phase 10E's** 60-call closure pilot, after four narrowly-scoped additive mapping edits, recovered SS-M/SR-M to 86.7%/86.7% — a ~40-point recovery — at negligible (+3.4%/+3.6%) token cost.
- **Phase 11** caught and corrected two of its own misattributions in Phase 10E's residual analysis: both remaining "failures" were themselves Question-A (retrieval-absent), not Question-B, once checked against the actual `selected_field_ids` column rather than inferred from response text.
- **Phase 12** further refined Phase 11's own attribution: the timeline residual's correct field pair is `identity.age` + `life_context.important_life_events` (not `life_context.career_history`, which describes job history, not Pune-residency duration); the single most direct fact lives in an unregistered `_full_document_supplement` field.
- **Phase 13** validated both remaining fixes deterministically (zero cost, real resolver calls, no LLM): each affects exactly 1 of 30 corpus scenarios, 0 unintended selections, 0 restricted-field impact, fully deterministic.
- **Phase 14** then closed the friends gap for real (2/2 CORRECT in both B and X) — confirming retrieval was the entire cause for that scenario.

**Explicitly stated: after Phase 14's friends-fix confirmation, no currently-unresolved failure in this evidence chain is known to be caused by missing retrieval.** The one scenario that remained partially unresolved after retrieval was closed (the timeline scenario) transitioned, at that exact point, into Question-B territory — this is the load-bearing transition the entire Phase 15–22 chain investigates.

## 2. Model-behavior / Question-B evidence

The transition and its evidentiary character, kept strictly separated by evidence type:

- **Before Phase 14**: zero confirmed Question-B failures existed anywhere in the Phase 10D/10E/11/12 dataset. Every failure examined, once checked against `selected_field_ids` directly, traced to a retrieval cause.
- **Phase 14 (observed behavior)**: `timeline/B` — the model stated "about three years" despite `identity.age`(27) and the "moved at 21" fact being confirmed present in the exact `selected_field_ids` for that call. This is an **observed behavior** fact: a wrong answer occurred with the needed facts present. It is not, by itself, mechanism evidence or causal evidence.
- **Phase 15 (mechanism evidence, not causal)**: a real, source-verified structural difference was found (B's renderer never labels top-level scalars) — this is **mechanism evidence**, a candidate explanation grounded in source code, not a demonstration that the mechanism actually operated. Phase 15 explicitly preserved a competing, equally-source-grounded explanation (27−24=3 milestone confusion).
- **Phase 16 (one observation, not causal)**: a single controlled call using a labeled rendering produced a correct answer where the unlabeled rendering had failed. This is **one additional observed-behavior data point**, consistent with the Phase 15 mechanism, but n=1 and not causal evidence — Phase 16's own document explicitly states this ("not proof of causation... a single call cannot rule out that B would have occasionally answered correctly anyway").
- **Phase 19 (reproducibility, not causal)**: repeating the identical comparison 5×/5× showed the same direction (B unreliable, L reliable) — this is **reproducibility evidence** (the observed pattern recurs under controlled repetition), still not causal evidence, since no variable beyond labeling was manipulated in isolation yet.
- **Phase 20 (isolation, still not causal)**: introducing LA (age-only) and finding it reproduced L's improvement is **the first evidence that narrows the mechanism** from "labeling in general" to "labeling the specific relevant scalar" — this is a more precise **mechanism-narrowing** step, not a jump to causal proof. The design explicitly limits itself to "favors H1 over H2 in this run," never "proves H1."
- **Phase 21 (independent repeat, still not causal)**: a second, larger (n=10) sample on the same scenario reproduced the direction. This is **replication evidence** for the observed pattern, explicitly not treated as strengthening any causal claim beyond "the direction reproduces."
- **Phase 22 (generalization, tempering)**: a genuinely independent scenario (different fact pair, different derived value, different semantic domain, added entity-binding requirement) showed the same direction but a much smaller magnitude, with B itself performing far better at baseline. This is **generalization evidence that tempers rather than strengthens** the earlier findings — it does not retroactively invalidate Phase 20/21's own reported numbers (which stand as measured), but it constrains what can be concluded about how broadly the pattern applies.

**No phase in this chain treats repeated direction, provider-to-request equivalence, or a correct response as proof of causation.** This distinction is maintained explicitly in every one of the Phase 20/21/22 result documents' own "what this does NOT establish" sections.

## 4. H1 / H2 / H3 evidence map

No scores, probabilities, rankings, or declared winners are assigned anywhere below, per instruction.

### H1 — age-specific labeling contributes to derived-number performance

- **Supported by**: Phase 20's LA arm reproducing L's magnitude on the same calls; Phase 21's independent 10-call repeat reproducing the direction; Phase 22's independent scenario reproducing the direction (though at reduced magnitude).
- **Limited by**: Phase 22's much smaller effect size on an independent fact pair; B's own within-condition variance across runs (Phase 20: 40% → Phase 21: 20% → Phase 22: 80%, all on different scenarios/difficulty) shows the baseline itself is not stable, which limits how much any single gap can be attributed specifically to labeling rather than to scenario difficulty or ordinary variance.
- **Remains missing**: any test that varies difficulty while holding the fact pair constant, or vice versa, to separate "does labeling matter" from "does labeling matter more when the task is harder" — this specific interaction is *suggested* by comparing Phase 20/21 (large gap, hard task) against Phase 22 (small gap, easy task), but was never directly tested as its own hypothesis.

### H2 — broader labeling is required (beyond age alone)

- **Supported by**: nothing in the current evidence base — no run has shown L outperforming LA.
- **Limited by**: Phase 20's direct LA-vs-L comparison, where LA matched L's magnitude exactly (4/5 each) — the one run capable of distinguishing H1 from H2 did not favor H2.
- **Remains missing**: any run where L and LA are compared side by side on more than one scenario or more than n=5 each — Phase 20 is the only such comparison, and it is a single small sample.

### H3 — generation variance explains the observed differences

- **Supported by**: the instability of B's own baseline rate across Phase 20 (40%), Phase 21 (20%), and Phase 22 (80%, different scenario) — real variance clearly exists in this system. Phase 22's small (10-point) gap on an easier scenario is specifically consistent with the entire observed difference there being ordinary variance.
- **Limited by**: the LA-over-B direction has now held in four separate samples (Phase 20, 21, 22, and the original Phase 16/19 L-vs-B pattern) without a single reversal — pure variance with no underlying effect would be expected to reverse direction at least occasionally across four independent small samples, though this absence-of-reversal is not treated here as ruling H3 out.
- **Remains missing**: a formal reproducibility check on a scenario where B's baseline is deliberately held in the "unreliable" regime (like the Pune scenario) repeated enough times to distinguish a real effect from a persistently unlucky run of B — not performed at a scale beyond Phase 21's n=10.

**None of H1, H2, or H3 is declared established, refuted, or ranked above the others.** The evidence as a whole is consistent with H1 as the most-often-supported reading across four samples, while H3 remains a live, evidence-grounded qualifier rather than a dismissed alternative, and H2 has one piece of direct limiting evidence (Phase 20) and no supporting evidence anywhere.

## 5. Competing 27−24 explanation

**Preserved, unresolved, exactly as Phase 15 stated it.** The hypothesis: B's original "three years" error (Phase 14) may have been influenced by the unrelated age 24 (the corporate-job-departure age, embedded in the same `life_context.important_life_events` list as the "moved at 21" fact, present identically in every condition — B, X, L, LA) rather than reflecting a general failure to compute 27−21.

**What subsequent phases actually tested regarding this**: Phase 19 checked whether any of its 10 responses referenced "24" explicitly — none did. Phase 20 checked the same across 15 responses — none referenced "24." Phase 21 checked across 20 responses — none referenced "24," but noted that B's wrong answers spread across "two/three/four/five years" rather than clustering on "three," an observation offered without conclusion. Phase 22 checked across 20 responses on a *different* fact pair (27 vs. 29, not 27 vs. 24) — not applicable to this specific hypothesis by construction, though two of its wrong answers ("three years" on a 27-vs-29 pair, where the correct answer is 2) show the same style of small-magnitude arithmetic slip independent of any 24-anchoring, which is reported as a separate, informative observation, not as evidence against the 27−24 hypothesis.

**Explicitly not claimed**: that the absence of "24" in any response's visible text disproves the 27−24 hypothesis. A model's internal computation is not obligated to surface its intermediate reasoning in visible text; the absence of "24" in output is silent on whether "24" was used internally. This alternative remains exactly as unresolved as Phase 15 left it — no phase since has designed a test capable of distinguishing "the model silently anchored to 24" from "the model made an unrelated arithmetic slip."

## 6. Phase 22 significance

Phase 22 matters because it is the only experiment in this chain that varies the fact pair, the semantic domain, and the derived value simultaneously while keeping the labeling mechanism (LA vs. B) identical:

- **Independent fact pair**: `identity.age`(27) + Anjali's age(29), vs. the Pune scenario's `identity.age`(27) + "moved at 21."
- **Different semantic domain**: family/sibling relationship vs. residence duration.
- **Different derived value**: 2 years vs. 6 years.
- **Added entity-binding requirement**: the model must correctly attend to "older sister" (Anjali, 29) rather than "younger brother" (Arjun, 24) within the same rendered array — a demand the Pune scenario never had.
- **B already performs relatively well** on this new scenario (80% CORRECT) — far above its 20–40% rate on the Pune scenario in Phases 20/21.
- **Only a 10-point B-vs-LA difference** resulted (vs. 40 points in both prior phases).
- **Both conditions showed the same qualitative wrong-answer pattern**: every wrong response in both B and LA cited the correct source ages immediately before stating an incorrect small-magnitude gap — an isolated arithmetic-output slip, not a wrong-fact or wrong-entity error.
- **Entity binding was flawless**: 20/20 correct in both conditions — no conflation of Anjali and Arjun, no use of Arjun's age (24) in place of Anjali's (29).

**This tempers generalization** because it demonstrates that the large effect sizes seen in Phase 20/21 are not automatically portable to any derived-numeric scenario — they may be specific to scenarios where B's own baseline reliability is already low, leaving room for an intervention (labeling or otherwise) to show a large apparent gain. On an easier scenario with a high B baseline, the same intervention showed only a small gap that is itself plausibly explained by ordinary variance. Phase 22 does not retract Phase 20/21's numbers; it constrains how far those numbers can be extrapolated.

## 7. What is actually established

- Selective retrieval failures observed in Phase 10D (SS 46.7%, SR 43.3%) were largely attributable to three identifiable, narrow mapping gaps (family, friends, location).
- Those mapping gaps were subsequently closed for the tested scenarios (Phase 10E: 86.7%/86.7%), at negligible token cost.
- After Phase 11/12's corrections, zero confirmed Question-B failures remained in the Phase 10–13 retrieval evidence base — every examined failure traced to a retrieval cause once checked against `selected_field_ids` directly.
- A present-fact/model-use discrepancy was observed under B in the Pune-timeline scenario (Phase 14), with both required facts confirmed present via the same field-presence standard used throughout the retrieval work.
- A real, source-verified structural difference exists between B's and X's/L's rendering of top-level scalars (Phase 15) — B never labels them; L labels all of them; LA labels only `identity.age`.
- Age-only scalar labeling (LA) reproduced the direction of improvement over B on repeated tests of the Pune-timeline scenario (Phase 20, Phase 21) and matched full labeling's (L's) magnitude in the one run where both were compared directly (Phase 20).
- An independent sister-age-gap scenario (Phase 22) reproduced the same direction, but with a much smaller difference (10 points vs. 40 points), and with flawless entity binding in both conditions.
- Therefore, **the current evidence is consistent with a possible benefit from age-specific labeling on some derived-number tasks — particularly those where baseline (B) reliability is otherwise low — but does not establish a universal effect, does not establish causation, and does not establish that the effect generalizes at a fixed magnitude across derived-numeric tasks in general.**

## 8. What is NOT established

- **Universal benefit of labels** — not established; Phase 22 shows the effect size varies substantially by scenario.
- **Causal proof** — not established anywhere in this chain; every phase from 16 onward explicitly disclaims causal proof in its own "what this does NOT establish" section.
- **Superiority of labeled rendering as a general property** — not established; only two scenarios have been tested, both timeline-family derived-numeric tasks.
- **General improvement on ordinary scalar recall** — explicitly contradicted by Phase 17, where labeling showed no effect on 4 pure-recall scenarios (though this null result is itself explained by a ceiling effect, not by labeling having no value).
- **Elimination of generation variance as an explanation** — not established; H3 remains a live, evidence-consistent qualifier throughout, most strongly in Phase 22.
- **Resolution of the 27−24 alternative explanation** — not established; no phase has designed a test capable of confirming or refuting it, and the absence of "24" in visible response text is not treated as resolving it.
- **Evidence that all arithmetic errors in this system are caused by rendering/labeling** — not established; Phase 22's wrong answers occurred in both B and LA, and Phase 20's newly-observed B failure mode (hometown/current-city contradiction) did not recur and was never connected to labeling.
- **Evidence that LA should replace B in production** — not established or claimed by any document in this chain; every results document (20, 21, 22) contains an explicit statement that no production recommendation is being made.
